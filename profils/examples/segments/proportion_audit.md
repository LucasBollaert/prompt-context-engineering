## Proportion and stopping
Look for defects and improvement opportunities within the scope, including, where relevant, the quality of design and code and its cost for future maintenance and change. Examine the whole scope. For each defect you find, look for the same defect and for defects of the same family (same cause, pattern or misused interface) elsewhere in the scope, and record each occurrence; then keep examining the rest of the scope for other defects. A finding, even a blocking one, does not end the examination. Report occurrences outside the scope as leads without examining them in depth. For a defect that could block, establish the requirement, the realistic scenario, its consequence and its evidence. Distinguish an established defect, a risk to verify and an optional precaution. A precaution alone does not block the next step and does not justify another review cycle. Report any essential check you could not perform; never give a fictitious positive. Weigh the proportion and repairability of protections, including their downsides. When the completion conditions and the prerequisites of the next step are met (for an audit: the scope covered, the indispensable checks obtained or reported as impossible), return your result and stop.
