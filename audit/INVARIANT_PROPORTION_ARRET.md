# Operational invariant — proportion and stopping (v1.7 candidate)

Status: a normative requirement of the design and control setup, not the result of a scientific study. Applies to audit, design and correction missions and to hand-offs towards realisation, when the mandate includes these steps. The applicable policies, permissions, security requirements, legal obligations and qualification criteria remain the priority. Profiles may specify existing classifications and gates; do not replace them with this text. No new gate is created.

## Imperative rule
- A precaution alone (an additional safeguard without an established functional defect or security risk within the scope, or a mere preference) never on its own blocks realisation, delivery or an authorised step. Only an established blocking finding requires a correction cycle; the others are recorded, even if their severity is high. An indispensable qualification not performed forbids the step that depends on it, without being treated as a proven defect. Do not require theoretical perfection, universal zero defects or endless protections. Do not lower an objective or change the mandate to obtain a positive result.
- For each defect that could block, state the applicable requirement, the object and its state, the realistic scenario and the consequence, then the evidence (authorised reproduction or deduction from verified premises). Design evidence can be a verifiable textual contradiction; no existing code is needed to establish that a specification is contradictory. A concrete security risk can be established without carrying out an exploit. Severity, evidence, class and blocking are distinct.
- A plausible risk that is not established is not proof of a defect; describe the check that would settle it. An indispensable check not performed prevents the qualification of the dimension concerned; an unresolved potentially serious risk requires a suitable decision or check before any step that depends on it. Never equate "not examined" with "sound", nor arbitrarily reclassify a finding as a precaution. Do not postpone a truly indispensable prerequisite.
- Judge protections in both directions: an excessive protection can create a defect, and a so-called precaution can hide a functional or security requirement. Compare with the need, the risk covered, the controls already in place, repairability and cost. Prefer the simplest sufficient control; a complex solution can be justified if the alternatives do not meet the requirements.
- Proportion is not minimalism. This rule removes additions made for caution alone; it does not lower technical ambition. For design, development and technical audit, professional engineering quality is part of the expected result: reliable, readable, coherent with the existing design, testable, maintainable and easy to change. "Simplest sufficient" is measured against the real requirements, including the foreseeable cost of maintenance and evolution; it never justifies a fragile shortcut, duplication or a design that will be costly to change. A quality defect is judged like any other finding (concrete cost, evidence, blocking only if established). No architecture, method or pattern is imposed, and quality is not a separate gate.
- As soon as the entry conditions of the next step are met according to the approved procedure, the blocking findings are resolved and the checks indispensable to that step are obtained, stop the loop and proceed with the authorised step. Tests planned for acceptance after development are not required before development when they are not prerequisites. Record the options without giving them the value of a gate. If a blockage persists or a family of defects recurs, apply the existing checkpoint or step-back, or submit the reasoned disagreement to the person in charge; no automatic relaunch without a concrete question to resolve. A static conclusion is not an authorisation to act.

## Transmission to prompts
The designer integrates the applicable invariant into the text actually transmitted to the mission agent (or into a qualified fixed clause that is actually assembled), with the rule of evidence and the stopping outcome. Deterministic controls may impose technical constraints, but they do not replace the instructions on judgement and result. Do not inject the full policy into small unrelated requests. The specialised auditors apply this invariant to their own reports and check that it is transmitted to the target. For a business procedure that requires exact wording, take that wording from the authorised version, without adding a competing classification to it.

## Reusable generic formulation (to adapt to the contract)
"Look for defects and improvement opportunities within the scope, including, where relevant, the quality of design and code and its cost for future maintenance and change. Examine the whole scope. For each defect you find, look for the same defect and for defects of the same family (same cause, pattern or misused interface) elsewhere in the scope, and record each occurrence; then keep examining the rest of the scope for other defects. A finding, even a blocking one, does not end the examination. Report occurrences outside the scope as leads without examining them in depth. For a defect that could block, establish the requirement, the realistic scenario, its consequence and its evidence. Distinguish an established defect, a risk to verify and an optional precaution. A precaution alone does not prevent the next step and does not justify another loop. Report any essential check you could not perform; never give a fictitious positive. Assess the proportion and repairability of protections, including their negative effects. When the completion conditions and the prerequisites of the next step are met (for an audit: the scope covered, the indispensable checks obtained or reported as impossible), return the result and stop."

## Formulation for realisation (to adapt to the contract)
"Do the requested work within its scope, at the level of quality a professional review would accept: reliable, readable, coherent with the existing design, testable and easy to maintain and change; weigh the foreseeable cost of maintenance and evolution in your choices. Do not add protections, abstractions, refactorings or features for the sake of caution alone; report optional improvements separately. Preserve the requirements, the required tests and the security controls; any authorised change to a test must preserve its oracle and be justified. Choose freely the means needed for the result. If a real defect or a concrete risk prevents a safe completion, establish it, give the evidence, and return a blocked state or a partial result that conforms to the contract."

These formulations are neither reader contracts nor authorisations to execute. The generic threshold and the arbitration of findings are defined in `audit/CONTROLE_PROMPTS.md`; approved business gates take precedence. The example profiles in `profils/examples/segments/` use these two formulations as fixed segments.
